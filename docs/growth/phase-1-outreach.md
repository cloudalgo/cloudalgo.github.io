# Phase 1 outreach pack

Drafts for the proof-collection work: the AppExchange review path, two
directory profiles, and the testimonial ask. Voice follows the house rules:
first person plural, no em-dashes, no buzzwords, soft close.

Nothing here is copy for the site. It is what you send and paste elsewhere.

---

## 1. How AppExchange reviews actually arrive

Worth stating plainly, because it changes what the work is: verified project
reviews are not requested. Salesforce sends them.

The path is:

1. Log the project in the **Business Console** (Partner Community).
2. Set the project status to **Completed**.
3. Salesforce emails the customer contact you named a ten-question CSAT
   survey, scored 1 to 5.
4. The final question asks whether they want their written feedback published
   as a Verified Project Review on the CloudAlgo listing.

Two constraints to plan around:

- Work where CloudAlgo was a **subcontractor to another partner does not
  count** for CSAT.
- A maximum of **two projects per customer** count toward the score.

So the listing showing zero reviews is most likely a consequence of projects
never being logged, not of clients declining to review.

## 2. The note that goes out before the survey

Send this a day or two ahead of logging the project as Completed. A cold
Salesforce survey gets ignored; a survey somebody is expecting gets answered.

> Subject: A short Salesforce survey heading your way
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Salesforce sends a short survey after a partner closes out a project, and
> ours for [project] is about to go out. It is ten questions and takes a
> couple of minutes.
>
> The last question asks whether you are willing to have your comments
> published on our AppExchange listing. If you are happy with how the work
> went, saying yes there genuinely helps us. If you are not, I would rather
> hear it from you first.
>
> Either way, thanks for the last [duration]. Good project to work on.
>
> Sandeep

Keep it that short. Do not explain the scoring, and do not ask for a specific
rating; both read badly and Salesforce takes a dim view of the second one.

## 3. Testimonial ask

Separate from the survey, and worth doing at the same time. Every one of the
five case study records has an empty `testimonial` field, so there is a slot
already built and waiting on each detail page.

> Hi [Name],
>
> We are writing up the [project] work as a case study for our site. The
> client is anonymised unless you would rather not be, and nothing goes live
> without you seeing it first.
>
> If you have two or three sentences on how it went, we would use them on the
> page with your name and role. No pressure either way, and no rush.
>
> Sandeep

Name and role matter more than length. One sentence with attribution beats a
paragraph from "a client".

Free at clutch.co/get-listed, roughly ten minutes, and the profile is
mirrored onto The Manifest at no extra cost. Reviews are free to collect.

**Company name:** CloudAlgo

**Tagline:** Salesforce work your team can still maintain after we leave.

**Founded:** 2019

**Locations:** Jaipur, India

**Service focus (suggested split):**

- Salesforce consulting and implementation
- Custom software and AppExchange product development
- MuleSoft and system integration
- Cloud and data engineering (AWS, Heroku, Airflow)

**Overview:**

> CloudAlgo is a Salesforce Consulting Partner working with organisations in
> the US and Europe on CRM implementation, AppExchange product development,
> MuleSoft integration and Heroku engineering.
>
> We are a small senior team rather than a staffing bench. The work we take on
> tends to be the awkward middle: an org that has accumulated a decade of

> overnight.
>
> We also build and run four of our own products, including OrgVitals for
> Salesforce org health scanning and AlgoBridge for Salesforce to PostgreSQL
> sync, so a fair amount of what we know about maintaining a Salesforce org
> came from maintaining ours.
>
> What we optimise for is being unnecessary afterwards. Clients keep the
> conventions, the documentation and an org their own admins can change.

**Notable results to list:**

  faster queries

**Minimum project size and hourly rate:** your call. Both fields are filters
on Clutch, so leaving them blank removes you from filtered searches. Setting
them at the low end attracts the wrong enquiries.

## 5. GoodFirms profile

Also free. Same content as Clutch with two changes:

- GoodFirms weights service percentages heavily in its category rankings.
  Concentrate rather than spreading across six services: putting 60% on
  Salesforce consulting and 40% across integration and product development
  ranks better than six even slices.
- It has a separate portfolio section. Paste the five case studies in, with
  the figures. Clutch does not have a real equivalent.

## 6. Order of operations

1. Log the five projects in the Business Console, contacts confirmed.
2. Send the warm-up note to each contact.
3. Mark projects Completed, one at a time rather than all at once, so the
   surveys do not all land in the same week.
4. Claim Clutch, then GoodFirms.
5. Invite three of the same clients to leave a Clutch review, at least a week
   after the Salesforce survey so the two asks do not collide.
6. Testimonial asks alongside, for the empty `testimonial` fields.
